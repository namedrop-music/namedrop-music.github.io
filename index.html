NameDrop: Examining Artist Mentions in Music AI

Xirui Jiang1, Junwei Deng1, Luyang Zhang2, Beibei Li2, Chris Donahue2, Jiaqi W. Ma1
1University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign
2Carnegie Mellon University
Paper · coming soon Audio examples

Music generation platforms use artist names in prompts to restrict what users may generate. Similar rules could also determine which creators receive credit. These rules are simple to implement, but a name is only an indirect signal of the music a model can produce and the training data that contributed to it. NameDrop examines two questions: can artist-name restrictions be bypassed without using those names, and does assigning credit to the named artist reflect estimated training-data contributions?

On ACE-Step, name-free prompts recover much of the similarity achieved by named prompts. Our black-box search agent refines musical descriptions using audio similarity and feedback from an audio language model, and also produces accepted prompts for a commercial music model. In a separate retraining-based study, we estimate artists' contributions using data Shapley values. The named artist is the largest estimated contributor for only 54% of outputs and receives 18.5% of attribution-based credit on average. Together, these findings question the use of artist names alone as a basis for generation restrictions and creator credit.

RQ1. Artist-name restrictions

Name-free rewrites recover 91% of the gap between generic and named prompts on ACE-Step. NameDrop goes further by searching for musical descriptions using an audio similarity score and feedback from an audio language model.

RQ2. Who receives credit?

We fine-tune Stable Audio 3 small on 1,117 tracks by 50 artists, then estimate artist-level Shapley values for 200 outputs using 927 fine-tuned models. The named artist ranks first for 54% of outputs and receives 18.5% of attribution-based credit on average, compared with 100% under a name-only rule.

Credit shares are proportional to positive estimated Shapley values. Each output carries one unit of credit.
